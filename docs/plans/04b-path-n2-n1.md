# Books Four and Five — the long tail

**Tier:** Paid. **Status: not started, and deliberately thin.**

**Goal:** extend the reading, mining and production machinery of [Book Three](04-path-n3.md) with
harder and broader content, plus the discourse and nuance grammar that separates "can read the
news" from "can read a novel".

This doc exists so the shape of the ladder is complete and nobody plans as though it ends at Book
Three. It is intentionally short. **A detailed plan written now would be fiction** — these books
are years of content away, and every earlier book has changed shape in contact with reality. The
one thing worth recording is what does *not* change, so the engine is not rebuilt for them.

---

## 1. Why these two share a doc

Books Four and Five differ from each other in **content difficulty and volume, not in method**.
Both are: read widely at your level, mine what you do not know, produce about what you read, review
on a cumulative interleaved schedule. That is Book Three's machine, fed harder texts.

Splitting them into two design docs would produce two copies of the same document with different
word counts in the table. When one of them genuinely needs its own design — and the likeliest
reason is listening, see §4 — split it then.

## 2. What actually changes

| Dimension | Book Three | **Books Four & Five** |
|---|---|---|
| Text source | levelled corpus, assembled | **native material**, selected rather than assembled |
| Vocabulary | mining plus some curation | **mining almost entirely** |
| Grammar | connective and discourse | **register and nuance** — formal, literary, spoken; what a form *implies* |
| Kanji | the common set | the long tail, and readings rather than shapes |
| Reading goal | comprehension with flow | **speed and stamina** over long texts |

**The register shift is the real content.** By Book Four the learner knows what sentences mean and
not what they *signal* — the difference between 〜んじゃないか and 〜のではないでしょうか is social,
not semantic. This is the hardest thing to teach and the least served by existing tools, which is
the same argument that makes Book Three the flagship.

## 3. What must not change

- **No gamification, at any level.** The temptation grows as content thins and engagement mechanics
  look like the answer. They are not.
- **Mastery gates, never grades** (DR-020). A shrinking set of work left, not a verdict.
- **Verified content only** (from Book Three onward). No training-canonical sentences.
- **The SRS interface stays swappable** — `Scheduler` in `src/types.ts`, `now` always injected.
- **Checkpoints stay on a cadence** at meaningful boundaries, whatever a boundary means by then.

## 4. The one open question worth naming now

**Listening has no home in this plan.** Every book so far is reading, writing and recognition.
Audio output exists as a plan (VOICEVOX); audio *input* — comprehending unscripted speech — appears
nowhere, and it is the skill that most separates a Book Five learner from someone who can actually
live in Japanese.

It does not need solving now. It needs to not be forgotten, because if listening ever becomes a
book it will be the one that genuinely needs its own design doc, and possibly its own engine.

## 5. When to write the real plan

When Book Three ships and its reading library has real usage. Not before. The levelling pipeline's
behaviour on real learners is the input that makes a Book Four plan worth more than a guess.
