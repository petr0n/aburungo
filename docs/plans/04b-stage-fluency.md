# The fluency stage — the long tail

**Stage:** `fluency` (DR-033) — the fourth and last: foundation, building, reading, fluency.
**Status: not started.**

**Goal:** extend the reading, mining and production machinery of
[the reading stage](04-stage-reading.md) with harder and broader content, plus the register and
nuance grammar that gets a learner from "can read the news with effort" to DR-034's endpoint:
**operating in Japanese at roughly N2** — reading native material, handling work and daily life
unassisted, following unscripted speech.

Per DR-024 the learner never reads a JLPT label. Per DR-034 **the course ends at roughly N2** —
this is the last stage, and it is bounded, not open-ended. N1 was in this document's scope when it
was first drafted; §4 says what changed and why, rather than deleting it silently.

**This is not "Books Four and Five" (DR-033).** A book is a volume; a stage is a difficulty band.
How many books the fluency stage takes is an authoring-time question, the same open question the
reading stage leaves for itself — not something this doc fixes in advance.

**Tier:** unsettled, and more likely paid than not. The reading stage immediately before this one
plausibly needs more than the two free-tier book slots left after Book Two — see its own tier
discussion, [04 §10](04-stage-reading.md). If that is right, every book in the fluency stage lands
past the free tier's reach by the time it opens. Exactly where the line falls is still 04 §10's
open question, not a new one to answer here.

**Sizing (owner directive, 2026-08-20):** a book targets ~10 chapters of ~10 lessons — a ~100
teaching-lesson skeleton, same landing zone as every other book (DR-033). §3 gives the fluency
stage's grammar that skeleton. It stops at band level on purpose: the bands are themes and
registers, not situations or lesson lists. The band model is locked
([04 §3](04-stage-reading.md), signed off 2026-08-20), but the levelling pipeline's behaviour on
real learners is the input the real plan needs — detail below band level written now would be
fiction.

**What ~100 lessons can and cannot carry:** the lesson skeleton holds grammar, register and kanji
teaching. It cannot hold the vocabulary — the gap is roughly 2,300 words from the reading stage to
this one, and that volume comes from **mining in the reading library**, not from lessons. The
skeleton is the taught spine; the library is the bulk.

---

## 1. Why this is still one document

The fluency stage differs from the reading stage in **content difficulty, register, and volume —
not in method**. Both are: read widely at your level, mine what you do not know, produce about
what you read, review on a cumulative interleaved schedule. That is the reading stage's machine
([04](04-stage-reading.md)), fed harder texts.

This stays one document, thin below chapter-band level, until the reading stage ships and its
library has real usage (§7). A full plan now would be fiction about a stage that has not started
and a book count nobody has fixed yet (DR-034).

## 2. What actually changes

Cumulative scope, from the standard reference estimates (gap-finders, never authorities — no
official JLPT lists have existed since 2010). This stage is the last one (DR-034): its endpoint is
this course's endpoint, not partway to a further stage.

| Dimension | Reading stage | **Fluency stage** |
|---|---|---|
| Words (cumulative) | ~3,700 | **~6,000** — close to DR-034's course-end figure of 6,105 |
| Kanji (cumulative) | ~650 | **~1,000** |
| Text source | levelled corpus, assembled | **native exposition, selected** — news, articles |
| Grammar | connective and discourse | **formal-written register + keigo, produced** |
| Reading goal | comprehension with flow | **speed over exposition** |
| Output | compose-then-compare on readings | **formal register production** — requests, opinion prose |

**The register shift is the real content.** This stage's grammar is still *functional*: patterns
that do a job in a sentence (formal connectives, keigo, stance and hedging), and the learner must
produce them — not the recognition-only literary register that used to sit one stage further out,
before DR-034 closed the course at N2 (§4). This is the hardest thing to teach and the least
served by existing tools — the same argument that makes the reading stage the centerpiece of this
plan.

## 3. Fluency-stage chapter bands (provisional)

Bands, not commitments; the reading stage's pipeline meeting real learners will reshape these.
Checkpoints stay on the DR-021 cadence at band boundaries, using the reading stage's gate: *read
something you could not read at the start of this band*. Gates, never grades (DR-020). A
production checkpoint closes each book this stage turns out to need (DR-033).

1. **From spoken to written** — the だ/である line, nominalisation, the register of exposition
2. **Formal connectives** — how written argument hangs together
3. **Keigo in production** — honorific and humble forms the learner must use, not just parse
4. **News reading** — articles and reportage; the band where the library goes native
5. **Stance and hedging** — asserting, qualifying and denying politely
6. **Obligation and concession** — the formal pattern families of duty, exception, contrast
7. **Natural spoken register** — contractions, sentence-final nuance, what a form signals
8. **Exposition at length** — essays and columns; stamina over multi-page argument
9. **Kanji to ~1,000** — completing the common-use set; compound readings
10. **Formal written production** — requests, apologies, opinion paragraphs, compose-then-compare

This is one book-shaped skeleton (~10 bands of ~10 lessons), same shape as every other book
(DR-033). Whether the fluency stage needs exactly one book or more than one is the same open
question the reading stage leaves for itself (04, intro) — this skeleton is what the first book
in the stage holds regardless.

## 4. N1 — out of scope (DR-034), and what this section used to hold

This document originally gave N1 the same treatment as N2 above: a ten-band skeleton titled "Book
Five," covering literary and archaic register, editorial critique, fiction, idiom, near-synonym
discrimination, ceremonial and business formality, the kanji long tail, abstract and specialist
prose, and register calibration in production.

**DR-034 puts N1 out of scope**, on the product's own terms rather than as a compromise: its
distinctive vocabulary is largely literary, formal-written and low-frequency, and an app organised
around real-life situations has no situation that reaches most of it. Adding it would mean 3,880
words selected by an exam's tail rather than by anything a learner does. The course's endpoint is
a capability, not a book count: operating in Japanese at roughly N2.

That skeleton is struck, not silently deleted — this section is the record of it. Some of what it
named is not actually N1-exclusive and may resurface inside the N2-capped fluency stage as
authoring finds room for it — near-synonym discrimination and formal ceremonial register both
start well inside N2 — but that is a future authoring decision, not a standing commitment made
here.

**Corpus note, preserved because it outlives the cut:** Tatoeba skews conversational.
Formal-written and literary sentences, even at N2, may be thin in it. Aozora Bunko (public-domain
Japanese literature) is a candidate verified corpus if the fluency stage's library needs it — a
**new content source**, so it needs owner sign-off and a data-sources entry before anything is
built on it, same as it would have for the struck N1 material.

## 5. What must not change

- **No gamification, at any level.** The temptation grows as content thins and engagement
  mechanics look like the answer. They are not.
- **Mastery gates, never grades** (DR-020). A shrinking set of work left, not a verdict.
- **Verified content only** (from the reading stage onward, DR-033/[04 §8](04-stage-reading.md)).
  No training-canonical sentences.
- **The SRS interface stays swappable** — `Scheduler` in `src/types.ts`, `now` always injected.
- **Checkpoints stay on a cadence** at meaningful boundaries, whatever a boundary means by then.
- **No Hana dependency** (DR-023). Production runs through production checkpoints and
  compose-then-compare; nothing here assumes conversation comes back.

## 6. Listening — a floor now, a ceiling named

Listening finally gets a home, in two honest halves.

**The floor lives inside these books and costs almost no new engine.** Every verified sentence and
passage can carry pre-generated VOICEVOX audio (already planned), and Tatoeba's native-speaker
recordings cover a subset with real human speech. That enables listen-first formats on the existing
machinery: audio played *before* text in review, audio fill-in-the-blank (already on the roadmap),
dictation-style cloze, and a listen-then-read mode in the reading library. In this stage, exposition
passages heard at natural speed before reading them is genuine listening practice. These are lesson
and review *formats*, not a separate track.

**The ceiling is real and stays flagged.** TTS over written sentences is scripted, single-speaker,
clean audio. Comprehending unscripted, multi-speaker, natural-speed speech — the skill that most
separates a learner who has finished this stage from someone who can live in Japanese — cannot be
assembled from a sentence corpus, and DR-023 removes the interactive path. If listening ever
becomes a book, it will be the one that genuinely needs its own design doc, its own content
pipeline (licensed real audio), and possibly its own engine. It does not need solving now. It
needs to not be forgotten.

## 7. When to write the real plan

When the reading stage ships and its library has real usage. Not before. The levelling pipeline's
behaviour on real learners is the input that makes a real fluency-stage plan worth more than a
guess — including the one open structural question this doc leaves alone: whether the stage is
one book or several (§3).
